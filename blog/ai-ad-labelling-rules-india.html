AI ad labelling rules in India: what brands must do in 2026

Short answer: an AI ad needs a label in India when the synthetic part could change what a buyer believes about the product. A synthetic influencer needs one. A dreamy AI background does not. And some AI uses are banned outright, label or no label. Here is how the two rulebooks actually split the work.

Parth Agarwal3 Nov 20268 min read

Two sets of rules now govern AI content in Indian advertising, and they are aimed at different people. MeitY's amendments to the IT Rules, in force since 20 February 2026, tell platforms what to do with synthetic media. ASCI's guideline, dated 17 September 2026, tells advertisers when their own AI creative needs a disclosure. If you make or buy AI ads, the second one is the one written for you.

We make AI ads for a living, so we read the primary documents rather than the summaries. This is what they say.

Do AI-generated ads need a label in India?

Sometimes, and the test is about the consumer, not the tool. ASCI's guideline says labelling is mandatory where synthetically generated content "materially influences consumer decisions" and leaving the label off would mislead. It explicitly frames itself around consumer outcomes rather than regulating the technology, and it says it aims to avoid label fatigue.

ASCI gives brands three questions to decide:

  • Is the synthetic content central to the ad's persuasion, or does it materially influence the purchase decision?
  • Would the way it is used mislead people about the product's claims, performance or benefits if it were not labelled?
  • Would the absence of a disclosure create a false impression of what the product or service does?

If any answer is yes, label it.

Two rulebooks, two different targets

Dimension ASCI guideline MeitY IT Rules amendment
Who it binds Advertisers and their ads Intermediaries, meaning platforms
Date Dated 17 Sep 2026, effective three months after publication Notified 10 Feb 2026, in force 20 Feb 2026

AI-written text No label needed for ad copy Excluded from the definition
Label wording Flexible, examples given Clear and prominent, plus metadata where feasible

When is an AI label mandatory?

ASCI lists the cases where a label is required:

  • Synthetic influencers and ambassadors. An AI-generated face selling your product needs a disclosure.
  • Replicated likeness or voice, even with consent. A personalised message using a real person's cloned face or voice still needs a label.
  • Fabricated events, settings or situations that could change how people understand the product's performance.
  • Products that do not exist yet, such as a 3D render of an unbuilt housing project.
  • Synthetic sound that is the product's core feature, such as audio quality in a headphone ad.
  • AI-driven sponsored recommendations. When a chatbot suggests a product because the brand paid for it, the disclosure must read "Sponsored by" and the brand name.

When is no label needed?

ASCI is just as clear about what does not need one:

  • Minor enhancements: colour correction, lighting tweaks, noise reduction, routine blemish removal, formatting and copy refinement.
  • Background and ambient elements: decorative skylines, ambient music, jingles and crowd sound unrelated to the product's capabilities.
  • Fantastical elements nobody would mistake for reality. ASCI's own example is a car that turns into a flying spaceship.
  • Text and administrative uses: generating or polishing ad copy and preparing documents in good faith.
  • Accessibility: subtitles, captions, translations and audio descriptions.

This matters for creative strategy. A stylised, clearly fantastical AI film sits in very different territory from a photoreal AI person holding your product and talking about results.

What is banned even with a label?

ASCI says some uses breach its code regardless of disclosure:

  • Fabricated endorsements or testimonials that imply a real person used or approved the product when they did not.
  • Exaggerated results shown through AI visuals. ASCI's example is an AI body transformation from 100 kg to 70 kg in a weight-loss ad that does not reflect substantiated results.
  • Non-existent locations or facilities presented as real, such as a hotel that does not exist.
  • Unauthorised copyrighted work, deepfakes, or a person's likeness used without consent.

What should the label say?

There is no fixed wording. ASCI suggests labels such as "Audio/Video created using AI" or "Audio/Video enhanced using AI", accepts the labels ad platforms provide, and allows any alternative that accurately informs the consumer. Its existing rules on disclaimers still apply, so a label buried in fine print will not count as a disclosure.

What do MeitY's IT Rules add?

The IT Rules amendment introduced the term "synthetically generated information" and put the duties on platforms. According to Freshfields' summary of the final rules, platforms must label such content clearly and prominently, attach metadata or a unique identifier where feasible, and take down unlawful content within three hours of a court or government order, down from 36. Significant social media intermediaries, those with more than five million users, must ask users to declare synthetic content, verify the declarations and label what is confirmed.

Two later developments are worth tracking. On 8 October 2026, MeitY issued an advisory, reported by MediaNama, asking social media platforms to label or contextualise manipulated or synthetic content, particularly content presented as current events. And a draft amendment from April 2026 that would require labels to stay visible for the full length of a video was still a proposal as of October 2026.

For a brand, the practical effect is that platforms may ask you to declare AI content when you upload or run it. Answer honestly, because the declaration and the ASCI test point the same way.

A checklist for brands running AI ads

  1. Decide labels at script stage, not after the edit. Run the three ASCI questions on every concept before production starts.
  2. Never use AI to fake a testimonial or a result. No label fixes that.
  3. Get written consent for any real likeness or voice, and label it anyway when it is used for personalised messaging.
  4. Prefer clearly stylised treatments when you want AI's speed without a disclosure, and keep product claims real.
  5. Keep the label prominent and readable, and use the platform's own AI disclosure setting where one exists.
  6. Keep a record of which assets used synthetic content and why each one was or was not labelled.

What this means for AI ad production

The rules reward craft over shortcuts. The ads that get into trouble are the lazy ones: a photoreal AI doctor, an invented customer, a too-good-to-be-true result. The ads that sail through are built on real product truth and use AI for scale, style and speed. That is the line our AI ad creative work is built around, and it is why we treat the script as the place where compliance gets decided.

If you are planning AI creative for 2027, the cheapest compliance step is the one you take before generation: decide what the synthetic content is doing in the ad, and whether a buyer could be misled by it.
